🌱 Scaling Solar‑Powered Agriculture – ROGEAP Investment Opportunity

Karakunku Farm is a proven Gambian agritech and clean‑energy enterprise that uses solar‑powered automated drip irrigation, solar cooling/storage and solar lighting to help smallholder farmers produce more reliably, reduce dependence on fossil fuels, and preserve food after harvest. Since 2019, we have built more than seven years of practical experience and now serve 27 smallholder farmers.

We are requesting a US$25,000 ROGEAP Matching Grant (with a US$8,333 counterpart contribution) to scale our proven model. This investment will establish a 10‑acre solar service/demonstration footprint, enable up to 500 farmers to access solar irrigation technology, deploy 50 solar cooling/storage refrigerators and install 200 solar farm/community lights.

📖 About Karakunku Farm

Founded in 2019 and formally registered in 2020, Karakunku Farm is a locally owned enterprise led by Lamin Ceesay, who brings over five years of hands‑on farming and business experience. Our core mission is to combine agriculture with renewable‑energy technology to solve the linked problems of energy poverty, water scarcity, and post‑harvest losses.

Today, we employ 7 people and have a dedicated management team including a CEO, CTO, CMO, CSO and CAO. We have built strong community trust and a loyal customer base. Our farm is a living demonstration where farmers see solar technology working in real conditions – the most powerful marketing tool for rural adoption.

Vision: To become The Gambia’s leading provider of solar‑powered agricultural solutions, transforming rural communities through clean, affordable energy access.

Mission: To empower rural Gambian farmers with solar technology that enables sustainable, profitable agriculture, while operating a commercially viable enterprise.

⚠️ The Challenge We Solve

  • Energy poverty: Over 60% of rural Gambian households lack electricity; farmers spend 25,000–40,000 GMD annually on diesel for irrigation.
  • Food insecurity: The Gambia imports over 60% of its food; seasonal production leads to shortages and price spikes.
  • Post‑harvest losses: Without cooling, up to 40% of produce is lost before reaching consumers.
  • Knowledge gap: Farmers lack access to affordable, reliable solar products and trustworthy installation/maintenance services.

Karakunku Farm bridges these gaps by demonstrating, distributing, installing and maintaining solar productive‑use systems – all integrated with commercial vegetable farming.

💡 Our Integrated Solution

  • Demonstration: Our farm is a living showroom – farmers see solar irrigation, cooling, and lighting working in real conditions.
  • Distribution: We supply high‑quality solar equipment with flexible payment options through microfinance partners.
  • Installation & maintenance: Our trained technicians ensure systems operate reliably, with responsive after‑sales support.
  • Agricultural production: We produce fresh vegetables year‑round using solar‑powered drip irrigation, proving the commercial viability.
  • Training & capacity building: We equip farmers with the skills to operate and maintain their systems, ensuring long‑term impact.

This integrated approach builds trust, reduces adoption barriers, and creates a sustainable cycle of clean energy and food production.

📊 Market Opportunity & Customer Segments

Primary customers: Smallholder farmers, rural off‑grid households, women’s farming cooperatives, and community gardens in the Lower River Region.

Secondary customers: Local households, market vendors in Soma, retail shops, food businesses, schools, hospitals, and agribusinesses requiring consistent supply.

Competitive advantage: Our unique combination of farming expertise and solar knowledge, rural presence, demonstration farm, after‑sales support, and local trust gives us an unbeatable edge over urban‑based solar companies and seasonal farmers.

With rising demand for fresh produce, increasing fuel costs, and government support for renewable energy, our market is poised for significant growth.

⚙️ Operations & Production

Agricultural Products: Hot pepper, okra, onion, cucumber – all grown with solar‑powered drip irrigation.

Solar Products & Services: Solar water pumps, home systems, cold storage units, farm electrification packages, plus professional installation, maintenance, and training.

Production Process: Land preparation → planting → solar drip irrigation → crop management → harvesting → solar cold storage → distribution.

Solar Service Process: Customer identification → needs assessment → system design → sales & financing → installation → after‑sales support.

Our 10‑acre demonstration farm showcases the entire ecosystem, providing tangible evidence of the technology’s impact.

This investment is catalytic – it builds productive assets and customer capacity, enabling commercial revenue to sustain operations beyond grant funding.

🚀 Growth & Replication Strategy

0–12 months: Deploy irrigation, cooling & lighting; onboard 500 farmers; train users; establish monitoring and after‑sales systems.

1–3 years: Reach 700–1,000+ farmers through replicated service clusters, deepen group contracts, expand commercial partnerships.

3–5 years: Build a multi‑cluster Gambian network, standardise deployment packages, and expand the productive‑use solar portfolio.

5–7 years: Explore regional replication (ECOWAS markets) where standards and market conditions permit.

Our long‑term ambition is to make clean energy a normal part of agricultural production in The Gambia and beyond.
